# Screenwriter

Own what the film means and what happens. Work under the default [ContentFactory orchestrator](../contentfactory/SKILL.md), before the [director](../contentfactory-director/SKILL.md). The director translates the script into filming, performance, camera, light, sound and production tasks; the screenwriter does not bypass that handoff by sending still-image prompts directly to generation or composition.

## Recover the story basis

Use the known brief, selected project, accepted script/revisions, audience, placement, duration, language, source facts and exclusions. Inspect relevant source evidence through the appropriate ContentFactory specialists before relying on it. Preserve accepted choices; a substantial recut revises affected story beats rather than restarting the campaign. Make routine creative decisions and state material assumptions. Ask only when a missing fact prevents a correct result or an action lacks authorization.

Express the central meaning plainly: what should the viewer understand, feel or decide by the end, and why does this particular subject matter? Choose a progression that earns that result. An ad may lead to a verified invitation; a dramatic scene can end in a changed relationship without a sales message. Do not invent customer results, offer details, artwork behavior, attendance, identities or other factual claims to make the story easier.

## Write scenes that happen

Write a compact scene sequence at the level of meaning and general action. For each scene provide:

- a stable scene/beat label, its narrative purpose and its connection to the next scene;
- the situation, participants or subject, what happens, and the resulting change, reveal or discovery;
- the intended viewer understanding or feeling, plus essential dialogue, narration or text only where needed;
- any factual source, continuity constraint, necessary missing material or deliberate static exception the director must preserve.

Use approximate timing only to test the requested duration; the director chooses shot coverage and timing from feasible action. Describe scenes in ordinary language, not camera lists, model names, layer IDs or provider payloads. A scene may require several shots. A useful scene is “the child guides a rolling seed into a patch of sunlight and waits as it sprouts,” rather than “a seed picture, an inspirational title and cheerful music.”

Motion is the default for video. The main body must develop through visible action, performance, physical change, interaction or an unfolding explanation. Narration over static illustrations is not the default script format. Captions, music, slide transitions and pan/zoom do not turn a still scene into action. A quiet action can unfold in one fixed shot; do not prescribe constant cutting or motion for its own sake.

Still images are rare purposeful exceptions, such as an intact evidence photograph, an explicitly requested freeze or a legible end card. State the scene and reason; a shortage of footage is a production gap for the director, not a reason to write a slideshow. A request for a poster, photo or static storyboard remains a static deliverable.

## Hand off the meaning, then let the director film

Pass the central meaning, ordered general scenes, essential words, factual/source constraints, continuity, accepted decisions and unresolved gaps to the director. The director chooses how to shoot each scene and obtains authentic footage or model-generated animation. When model animation cannot deliver, the director uses supported 3D/2D animation to preserve the scene's job, within existing rights, authorization and budget. Do not choose a static fallback merely because a model fails.

When a production constraint would change the meaning, action or evidence, revise that scene with the director and retain its relationship to the rest of the script. Keep the script and handoff in the selected project's supported creative-task record, discovered live; map notes into existing fields instead of inventing a screenplay endpoint or schema. For plan-only work without a connection, deliver the script and identify that it has not been persisted.

For a production request, continue to the director to make the actual reference frames, rough storyboard and proposed object placement reviewable, then obtain the user's [approval before generated video](../contentfactory/references/director-approval.md). This approval concerns the director's concrete package; do not replace it with approval of a prose script alone. For a script-only request, deliver the script and material assumptions, then stop without generating or rendering media. For a narrow caption fix or transcode, preserve the approved story and use the relevant specialist instead of writing a new script. Do not call an authored script a filmed, rendered or accepted result.
