# Timecode and scene revisions

Use for requests such as removing a picture at 10 seconds, moving a caption, lengthening an intro, or replacing one episode shot. Preserve the accepted brief and other corrections; do not start a new advertising campaign workflow.

For a named scene or episode, recover its stable editorial ID and interval, and distinguish episode-local time from full-composition time before editing. Preserve neighboring episodes, source offsets, narration and caption clocks. Use supported scene metadata and composition commands rather than inventing an episode endpoint; if nesting/interchange is required, discover that capability first. Keep the updated scene purpose and composition pin in the creative task.

1. Restore the current creative task and composition revision. Resolve the timecode to the complete visible layer/scene interval, including transition overlap. Distinguish a photo clip lasting 1.5 seconds from one video frame. If only a flattened MP4 exists, report that editable state is absent. Importing it cannot restore original layers, obscured pixels or unavailable source footage. Establish a supported reconstruction/replacement plan and adequate authorized sources before promising to preserve the old edit; this may remain impossible with the available material.
2. Choose the requested temporal behavior. Removing a shot can leave a gap, close time with a ripple, or replace that interval. Infer a replacement when uninterrupted voiceover/timing has been explicitly accepted; state that choice. Ask only when ambiguity materially changes the result. Never delete the source asset to remove its occurrence.
3. Check adjacent source handles, resolution, aspect, actual motion and speech boundaries. Extend genuine usable footage, not a freeze or an unnoticed repeat. When a shot is extended, extend its relevant context caption and adjust the two transitions too; dialogue subtitles remain tied to speech. For an intro, verify the animation reaches its intended resolved image and leaves enough reading time before the dissolve; do not guess the cut from a mislabeled thumbnail. Preserve complete speech and source-picture/audio offsets.
4. Submit the minimal atomic edit by stable IDs and revision. Validate and render the committed revision through ContentFactory. Carry forward all unrelated accepted choices. Tiny edits should not require recoding a local renderer or rechecking every source.
5. Check the changed interval and both joins in the exact output. For captions, include the intended phone-size framing and protected face/mouth area; for motion/jitter, inspect a temporal interval or exact consecutive frames. These focused editing checks supplement the [mandatory Gemini final quality audit](quality-audit.md): submit the complete revised video when <=180 seconds, or ordered video sections covering the whole export when longer. A tiny edit is not an audit exemption. Never treat a manifest offset or hash as perceptual lip-sync/listening proof.
6. Read the completed Gemini report and coverage, resolve material findings and audit the corrected final output. Save the new composition revision/output receipt, audit evidence and a brief change note in the creative task. Deliver the artifact for the user's review, not a claim that subjective acceptance is complete. If the required audit is blocked, label it incomplete with the specific prerequisite.

Before delivery ask internally: Did I remove the entire offending shot? Did I introduce a freeze, repetition, blank band, orphaned caption, abrupt dissolve or truncated sentence? Does the receipt actually prove the pipeline I say ran?
