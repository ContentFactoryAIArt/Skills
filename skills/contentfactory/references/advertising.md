# Advertising: from source evidence to a coherent edit

Read this for a new advertisement, a substantial advertising recut, or a correction whose meaning depends on the story. Use the shared [ContentFactory orchestrator](../SKILL.md) and the relevant domain skill for execution. For new ads and substantial creative recuts, establish or reuse the [screenwriter's script](../../contentfactory-screenwriter/SKILL.md) first, then the [director's staging plan](../../contentfactory-director/SKILL.md); this reference supplies the advertising evidence and edit safeguards. A small timecode correction does not require rebuilding the whole campaign.

## Choose the right size of work

- **New ad or new objective:** establish the brief and sources, have the screenwriter define the meaning and general scenes, then have the director stage the action, performance, blocking, camera, lighting and sound, obtain missing moving coverage, and create the composition. Use the director's separate exhibition treatment for exhibition advertising.
- **Substantial recut:** read the existing brief, composition revision and earlier feedback; change the affected story beats and preserve accepted choices.
- **Timecode edit:** identify the actual clip/layer and surrounding boundaries at the saved revision. Make the smallest meaningful edit, render, and inspect that interval plus the final export's technical result. Reuse unchanged audio evidence when an actual comparison supports it.

Do not turn every correction into new research, generation, a large contact sheet, or another paid model review. Do not make the user restate known project decisions.

## Brief and evidence before selection

Resolve the advertised action: attend an event, join a community, buy a service, or another stated goal. Record the audience, placement/aspect ratio, language, duration target, verified offer/date, call to action, chosen sources and important exclusions in the project's creative task. Distinguish user-confirmed facts, source-supported facts and unknowns; omit unsupported venue, attendance, performance or identity claims.

Inspect the supplied material before choosing a story. Collect enough relevant context to understand it, rather than maximizing collection volume. A Telegram forum is not a channel: discover the current collector, inspect returned rows, date range, topic coverage and truncation, and report a bounded sample honestly. Empty typed results do not establish an empty community.

Maintain a concise source map with ContentFactory asset IDs/pins, provenance, actual media type, usable source ranges, quality and reuse scope. A video container may hold only still photos. Public availability does not prove commercial reuse rights. Label generated/illustrative material and earlier-event footage when needed to prevent a false impression; do not present community members' work as the advertiser's own customer project.

Compare available coverage with the staging plan. Proactively search, import or generate missing material through the relevant ContentFactory specialists within existing authorization and budget; do not wait for the user to request each shot. Track each gap's purpose, acquisition route and result. When rights, access or spending authority blocks a particular asset, continue independent work and use a truthful alternative or report that specific limitation.

## Make every beat earn its place

Use a compact board with these fields, keeping it in the project's creative task or another supported project record:

| Timeline beat | Source asset/range and motion route | Why this shot now? | Spoken/text meaning | Cut and quality risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opening | Exact source range | Gives the viewer a reason to continue | One clear promise or question | Does the visual action reach its readable payoff? |
| Evidence/context | Distinct relevant shots | Supports the promise | Explains the connection when pictures alone cannot | Is this a still, a repeat, or misleading context? |
| Invitation/action | Verified invitation and destination | Makes the next step clear | Date/offer and usable CTA | Does the text remain readable long enough? |

Adapt the shape to the material. Dynamic editing means meaningful change and movement, not an arbitrary maximum shot length. Let an opening action complete and its result register before cutting. Avoid both an unrelated static opener and a generated motion fragment that ends before its payoff. Check actual source timestamps and exported timestamps; a seek followed by playback/wait is not an exact-frame sample.

Vary people, shot size and visual action when the sources support it. Do not repeatedly return to one speaker angle to fill gaps. A work-photo sequence needs an editorial reason and sufficient context; motion effects do not supply meaning. A concise line such as “Работы мастеров из сообщества” can connect images to the story, but remove an unwanted insert when the user asks rather than retaining it behind new styling.

The main body must use moving scenes. For still sources that may truthfully be animated, obtain subject/scene movement through supported video models; when that route cannot deliver, stage genuine 3D/2D animation through supported ContentFactory operations. A flat photo pan/zoom with narration, animated text or music is not the fallback. Preserve work/evidence photos intact when animation would imply unverified behavior; seek authentic moving coverage or a clearly identified illustrative scene instead. Any retained still needs a specific purpose and interval, such as a source-evidence insert or readable end card. Missing footage and failed generation alone do not justify a slideshow.

## Keep voice and picture coherent

Preserve complete spoken thoughts; locate a natural sentence/clause boundary before trimming. A transcript or Gemini time estimate is semantic evidence, not sample-accurate alignment. Speech can continue over cutaways, but when the person returns on screen, source-picture time must match source-audio time. Extending an intro must not restart the speaker picture from the wrong point or silently discard the beginning of the invitation.

For “remove the image at 10 seconds,” distinguish a single frame from the entire insert. Determine whether the request calls for a ripple deletion, a replacement, or extending neighboring footage from current context. If keeping the soundtrack timing is appropriate, use genuinely available neighboring motion rather than freezing a frame or duplicating a shot. Adjust the captions and transitions whose intervals change. Preserve accepted content outside the request.

## Protect quality and readability

- Prefer the best authorized original. Probe resolution, duration, streams and source quality before editing; export bitrate cannot restore a poor source. Do not hide visibly degraded footage behind a high-resolution output setting.
- Fit landscape material intentionally in a portrait ad. A crop must retain the subject; a blurred background must not leave distracting dark emptiness or unrelated decorative lines. Choose a better shot when the available treatment cannot serve the story.
- Use the requested caption language consistently. Place text away from mouths, useful action and platform overlays; choose contrast, weight and size for the target viewing size. A thin lower gradient can help, but its suitability depends on the actual frame.
- Authored overlays should have stable positioning and deliberate entry/exit. Check changed frames for text jitter, blackouts and clipping. Do not infer smooth motion from widely spaced stills.
- Cut on meaningful action or speech. Use short transitions where they improve the join; do not apply dissolves indiscriminately or blur through a key expression, label or completed intro image.

## Proportionate review and delivery

Use ContentFactory validation and the applicable domain instructions before rendering. Track the saved composition revision, render receipt and exact output asset. Inspect the final output rather than only a preview. Do not substitute a local renderer because the platform is inconvenient; follow the shared workflow if a required capability is unavailable.

For a small visual edit, inspect a few points around the changed interval, including its joins and caption entry/exit. For a new ad or substantial recut, review the story as a sequence as well as representative technical samples. Match the review method to the issue: stills can reveal text placement, but continuous playback is needed to judge pacing and many motion problems. Say when playback/listening or device-size readability was not available.

Request a paid visual/audio analysis only when it answers a specific unresolved question that the available evidence cannot answer, within authorization and the agreed cost scope. Keep existing receipts; reconcile uncertain results before retrying. Honor requests for direct user review. Do not upload private visuals merely because an audio-only review was authorized.

Treat review and production budgets separately. A $0 review allowance does not authorize paid music generation or define a total production ceiling. Check actual charging effects; "native" alone does not mean an operation is free.

At these three points, explicitly challenge the current choice internally and record only decisions that matter:

1. **Planning:** “Why this shot now? What evidence changes this choice?” Is the audience's next action clear, and does the board support it?
2. **Changed interval:** Did the actual requested defect disappear? Did this introduce a frozen/repeated shot, clipped phrase, empty caption area or bad join?
3. **Final:** Do the exact output and receipts support the claims? What remains a subjective or unverified judgment for the user?

Deliver the exact output, the specific change and the review actually performed. Retain the editable composition and project records for the next correction. These checks reduce avoidable rework; they do not guarantee that the user will like a creative result.
