# Review a directing decision, then the actual cut

Use this for a treatment, rough cut, final export or creative complaint. The standards are diagnostic judgments, not a scientific beauty score. Research context: [cinema](research-cinema.md), [platforms](research-platforms.md), [creative agents](research-agents.md).

## Match the evidence to the claim

| Evidence available | Can support | Cannot establish by itself |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Brief, script, beat/shot plan | Specificity, intended causality, feasibility, planned continuity and sound | Actual performance, image quality, rhythm, intelligibility |
| Storyboard/stills/contact sheet | Framing, identity/appearance at samples, typography, palette | Continuous movement, lip sync, complete action, musical timing |
| Transcript or analysis summary | Spoken content and a lead for inspection | Exact word timing, tone, mix, source truth, full listening |
| Probe, waveform, validation/render receipt | Reported streams, dimensions, technical checks, job state | Persuasion, narrative clarity, pleasing sound or creative acceptance |
| Continuous playback/listening of exact export | Observed motion, joins, performance, sound and sequence-level rhythm under those conditions | Audience approval, conversion lift, every device's behavior |
| Audience/campaign evidence | Observed responses in the sampled setting | Universal effectiveness or a causal claim without a suitable comparison |

Record what artifact/revision and ranges were actually inspected, how, and what remains unknown. Do not turn unavailable playback into a passing result. Reuse valid unchanged evidence; a small caption fix need not trigger a new full paid soundtrack review.

## Diagnose before prescribing

| Complaint | Inspect | Possible repair, chosen by evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “Boring/static” | Does the image contain a developing action, interaction, transformation or spatial reveal, or only text/audio over stills and pan/zoom? | Obtain applicable moving footage or animate the still through a suitable video model; if unsuitable/unavailable, author 3D/2D action. Preserve intentional performed stillness and rare recorded still inserts. |
| “Repetitive” | Are consecutive shots doing the same narrative job, even with different people? | Remove/merge redundant beats; obtain distinct evidence or a new stage of the action. Changing zoom alone may repeat the problem. |
| “Random/AI-looking” | Are subject, identity, product, physics, geography and style coherent? | Restore anchors and plausible actions; simplify an unstable interaction; replace a mismatched clip. Attractive frames do not excuse inconsistency. |
| “No story” | Can the viewer follow what changes and why? | Restore missing setup or consequence; reorder for causality/association; obtain the bridge rather than explain everything in voiceover. |
| “Too busy” | Do motion, text, speech and music compete for attention? | Establish hierarchy; hold on a key action; remove decorative transitions and redundant copy. |
| “Cheap/generic” | What makes the concept specific, and are source quality/performance/art choices coherent? | Choose a distinct premise/material/performance; source correct product/setting detail. Export resolution cannot recover a poor source. |
| “Sound doesn't work” | Actual listening: diction, timing, masking, space, abrupt joins and musical development | Recast/re-record if authorized, rephrase, change cue timing, repair transitions or rebalance the mix. A loudness number is only one check. |

Make notes as **moment → observed problem → intended effect → concrete change → verification**. Example: “The opening cuts before the latch closes, so the claimed ease of use has no visible result. Obtain/extend the complete closure, preserve its click, then review that beat in motion.” Avoid “make it more cinematic” as a revision instruction.

## Three review moments

**Treatment:** recover the screenwriter's meaning and general scenes before judging the director's filming plan. Test specificity, audience, truthfulness and ability to produce essential moving shots. Check the acquisition plan actually closes the story gaps, with model animation and a feasible 3D/2D alternative where still sources need motion. A producer challenges cost/rights/schedule; the director resolves filming choices and returns changes of meaning to the screenwriter through the orchestrator. Do not add endless role-play debates.

**Rough timed sequence:** check setup/payoff, spatial logic, performance, pauses, change between beats, sound structure and provisional text readability. Test the uncertain essential shot before finishing the whole film. Separate missing footage from an editing problem. When useful, compare two meaningfully different constructions rather than two color grades.

**Exact final export:** watch and listen through the sequence when supported, then inspect specific joins, captions, subject fidelity, interruptions and technical defects. Confirm the planned visible actions actually develop; a successful render, animated text, soundtrack or drift across a still cannot pass motion acceptance. Check each still insert against its recorded purpose and interval; a narrated slideshow needs an explicit user request. A muted pass can test visual comprehension for relevant placements; an audio-only pass can reveal sonic structure. Neither replaces the combined experience. Check the actual target framing and reading size, not just a large desktop preview.

## Judging a plan without pretending it is a finished film

Use anchored judgments per dimension: **missing/contradictory**, **generic or incomplete**, **specific and executable**, or **demonstrated in the artifact**. Only the final category requires observed media. Do not average away a false claim, unavailable essential shot, missing authorization or unreviewed output.

Evaluate:

1. Audience/intent: the intended response is clear and appropriate to the form.
2. Dramatic/persuasive mechanism: each beat changes something meaningful.
3. Staging/performance: actions and spatial relationships can be executed.
4. Visual/sound identity: choices form a coherent, subject-specific system.
5. Rhythm/continuity: entry/exit and holds serve perception and consequences.
6. Production: essential gaps have viable routes, and authorized routes are acted on in creation mode.
7. Evidence/budget: claims, provenance, costs and limits are represented honestly.
8. Delivery/review: the requested work is carried through and proof levels are explicit.

For an independent review, provide the request, relevant raw material, plan/cut and constraints. Do not tell the reviewer the desired verdict. Ask for concrete high-impact defects with timestamps or beat IDs. Use the critique to make a bounded revision; record why a suggested change was accepted or rejected. Model agreement is not user acceptance.

## Stop conditions and handoff

Finish when the requested deliverable exists, relevant defects are addressed, the required technical checks and available creative review have been performed, and limitations are stated. If a required capability/authorization is missing, deliver the useful completed portion with the exact unresolved action. Never call a draft accepted just because a score crossed a threshold.

For a new paid attempt, specify what will change and why it could fix the observed failure. Reconcile an uncertain earlier receipt before resubmitting. If the same cause remains or the budget is exhausted, change the route or report the specific blocker. Do not spend indefinitely to chase an undefined ideal.
