# Directing structures and executable advertising treatments

These are adaptable structures, not templates to fill mechanically. The screenwriter establishes meaning, progression and general scenes first; the director uses the matching structure here to make those scenes filmable. Changes of premise go back through the ContentFactory orchestrator to the screenwriter. Combine structures only when the combination has a clear function. Durations below are illustrative planning choices, never platform limits or performance guarantees. Source rationale is in [platform research](research-platforms.md), [cinema research](research-cinema.md) and [exhibition research](research-exhibitions.md).

Every form below defaults to moving scenes. Stage complete actions, interactions, transformations or spatial reveals. Animate still source material through a suitable discovered video model; when that route is unsuitable or unavailable, use supported ContentFactory 3D/2D animation. Static pictures with text and audio, or pan/zoom/transitions alone, do not implement these treatments. Keep still inserts rare, purposeful and bounded; factual claims still require authentic applicable evidence.

For generated moving scenes in any form, prepare/select and inspect actual reference frames first, then show a rough storyboard and proposed object/character placement to the user and obtain explicit approval before generating video or a motion prototype. This gate also covers newly authored 3D/2D animation; research and authorized reference/still preparation can proceed beforehand. Prefer a compatible configured Seedance 2.5 entry discovered live, with a supported alternative explained when necessary. See [production](production.md) for the reviewable package and approval scope.

## 1. Discovery Short: event → change → earned payoff

Use when one compact action or idea can satisfy a swipe-feed viewer. Start inside an intelligible event or a relevant unusual relationship. Establish what matters, develop it and pay it off. The opening should belong to the film; an unrelated surprise followed by generic sales copy breaks the promise.

**Direct it:** choose a readable action, subject scale and focal point for the actual vertical frame. Preserve the moment that explains the result. Let a reaction or changed object state complete the beat. Sound can announce an offscreen event or punctuate the payoff. A loop is optional; it must not erase the conclusion.

**Produce it:** acquire the missing before/action/result or context shot. A different angle of the same static presenter may add no information. **Review:** can a first-time viewer identify the event and payoff without an explanation from the editor? Do not transplant a fixed hook duration into every format.

## 2. Demonstration/performance ad: relevant difficulty → observable intervention → credible result → next step

Use when a product or service solves a demonstrable problem. The intervention must be specific to the actual offer. Show conditions and limitations that materially affect the inference; do not imply controlled proof from unrelated before/after clips.

**Direct it:** stage the task with the real product, a clearly located obstacle, a complete critical action and a legible result. Give the demonstrator an intention such as checking, fitting or comparing, rather than performing generic enthusiasm. Hold a continuous view when it improves evidential clarity. Inserts should reveal the mechanism, not hide whether it worked.

**Produce it:** seek authentic process/result footage or a shoot brief before synthetic proof. Generation may illustrate an explained mechanism if labeled appropriately. **Review:** what benefit can this exact sequence substantiate? Is the invitation understandable and supported?

## 3. Brand commercial: specific situation → expressive transformation → distinctive brand association

Use for memory, feeling or identity when immediate conversion is secondary. Find a particular relationship, ritual, tension or sensory experience. The brand/product should participate in the transformation rather than appear as a logo pasted onto an interchangeable montage.

**Direct it:** define the starting and ending states; stage the action bridging them. Coordinate costume, material, lighting, environment, movement and music around that change. Leave space for interpretation when the brief wants it. A brand film can be lyrical without being random.

**Produce it:** prototype the expensive transformation and obtain the exact brand assets/product geometry. **Review:** does the chosen visual/sound system belong to this subject? A logo-replacement thought experiment can expose generic direction; it is a diagnostic, not a prohibition on abstract work.

## 4. Music video or music-led advertisement: musical world → developed motif → transformation or release

Listen to the actual selected music when available. Map phrases, arrivals, vocal emphasis, silence and shifts in texture; a tempo value alone is not a musical analysis. Align the artist/product's presence with that structure.

**Direct it:** design a performance, choreography, visual motif or narrative that develops. Specify movement through space, gesture, gaze, wardrobe and environment, and the change at a musical turning point. A held performance may serve the song better than illustrative coverage of every lyric. Do not cut on every beat by default.

**Produce it:** obtain authorized track/stems, performance coverage and visual elements matching the motif. Test difficult synchronization or interactions early. **Review:** does the image deepen the song/brand experience, or merely accompany it? Check the actual sound-picture relation continuously.

## 5. YouTube explanation/essay: question → evidence → complication → changed understanding

Use when the viewer came to learn, investigate or explore. Make a truthful promise about the question. Arrange discoveries so each section changes what the viewer understands; do not use arbitrary suspense to postpone an answer already earned.

**Direct it:** stage demonstrations, useful diagrams and relevant observation. Decide when a presenter establishes trust and when the viewer needs to inspect evidence instead. Give complex actions enough time. Chapters, jokes and stylistic interruptions should support the inquiry rather than reset it.

**Produce it:** actively collect the sources, demonstrations, context and counterevidence the argument needs. Decorative stock does not establish a factual claim. **Review:** can the viewer follow how evidence supports the conclusion? Retention dips/spikes need contextual diagnosis; they do not directly measure truth or quality.

## 6. Dramatic scene/short film: relationship or condition → consequential experience → altered state

Use for fiction, microdrama and narrative ads. For causal drama, an objective, resistance, action and consequence often help. For observational or lyrical work, a changing perception or relationship may organize the scene without overt conflict.

**Direct it:** locate the characters, what they attend to, what they conceal, and how their distance or behavior changes. Give actors playable actions. Choose coverage and sound perspective that reveal the scene's point of view. A pause or offscreen event can carry the turn. Track consequences across serialized scenes.

**Produce it:** maintain identity, wardrobe, prop/action state and geography across generated shots. Source/capture difficult emotional or physical interactions when generation cannot sustain them. **Review:** is the intended change perceptible without explanatory captions? Is a continuity break purposeful and legible?

## 7. Testimonial/documentary invitation: situated person → specific experience → grounded evidence → appropriate invitation

Use when credibility depends on a real person or event. Preserve the speaker's meaning and material qualifications. Context and observed activity should support, not manufacture, the relationship between testimony and evidence.

**Direct it:** attend to listening, pauses, gaze and surrounding activity. Use relevant cutaways when they add evidence or context; do not cover every human hesitation with stock. Ask for specific lived examples rather than scripted generic praise when capturing new material.

**Produce it:** obtain real supporting material and appropriate permissions. A generated face/reaction is not a customer testimonial. **Review:** what would the juxtaposition lead a reasonable viewer to believe, and is that supported?

## 8. Contemporary exhibition advertising: encounter → meaning → visit experience → practical invitation

This is a distinct directing treatment for contemporary exhibitions, with art/design and trade-show variants. “Contemporary” does not imply neon, glitch effects or immersive projection. Start from the actual works, curatorial idea, professional relevance and audience.

### Establish the visit promise

Identify the exhibition's name/edition, organizer, audience, venue/city, dates, admission/registration destination and the experience genuinely on offer. Distinguish the main show from associated events, public areas, timed performances and separately ticketed activities. Confirm logistical facts before release; official pages can contain mixed edition information. Do not invent ticket scarcity, accessibility, exhibitors, visitor counts or a closing date.

Choose the appropriate branch:

- **Art/design/museum:** a distinctive encounter with a work leads to its curatorial question, a meaningful relationship with another work or a visitor, and an invitation to experience the show. Preserve attribution, appearance and human scale. Looking can be active; do not invent touch/interactivity.
- **Immersive/participatory:** reveal what the visitor can actually do and how space responds. Preserve enough spatial context to distinguish an experience from an attractive screen image. Do not promise an effect that exists only in a generated concept.
- **Trade/professional:** begin with a relevant professional problem or discovery, show an authentic demonstration or technical encounter, establish why this gathering is useful, and invite registration or a specific meeting. Distinguish exhibitor recruitment, visitor acquisition and booth appointments. Confirm eligibility.

### Direct a short exhibition advertisement

An original 25-second example for a hypothetical design exhibition follows. Times are provisional; real speech, movement, media and placement determine the final timing.

| Beat | Staging, camera and performance | Sound and text | Material needed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Encounter, about 0–5 s | A kinetic object completes one readable movement; reveal a person at an appropriate distance to establish scale. Keep the movement and response in a coherent space. | Begin with the object's actual sound if usable; one concise curatorial question. | Authorized footage of the named work and its whole action; inspect for flicker and clipped motion. |
| Meaning, about 5–11 s | A material detail leads to the maker indicating that feature; frame the hand/object relation rather than a generic talking head. | One complete, verified maker phrase or concise interpretation. | Correct artist/work attribution; a licensed detail or authorized capture. |
| Experience, about 11–18 s | A visitor shifts viewpoint around a second work; reveal a new relationship, not another indistinguishable close-up. | Room tone and a purposeful musical development; no invented visitor quote. | Authentic space/visitor coverage; release/reuse scope where required. |
| Invitation, about 18–25 s | Return to a recognizable work or a live view of the space, retaining a quiet area for information while a visitor's movement or the work's actual action continues. | Confirmed title, venue/city, dates and ticket/registration action with enough reading time; a separate still end card is a brief purposeful exception. | Current official logistics, applicable moving coverage and brand assets. |

**Producer action when footage is missing:** inspect the organizer's authorized project library, current press kit and other suitable sources; obtain usable current-edition detail/space shots through the permitted research/import path. If a conceptual transition is needed, generate it without presenting it as an actual exhibit. If only last year's material exists, label its status and avoid implying unconfirmed works are returning. A shoot request should name the missing action, framing, duration/handles, lighting/access restrictions and attribution needs—not simply ask for “more content.” Execute available authorized acquisition before asking the user to do it.

For a trade-show adaptation, replace the kinetic artwork with a specific demo, the maker statement with a substantiated technical insight, and the gallery encounter with a relevant meeting/session. End with the correct audience eligibility and registration or booth appointment. Do not substitute crowds and handshakes for demonstrated professional value.

### Exhibition-specific review

Can a viewer explain what makes this show different and what they would see/do? Are the featured works, spaces and people part of the advertised edition? Does framing/color preserve the work's appearance? Is the invitation accurate, readable and usable? Are music, imagery and capture permissions appropriate? Do sensory/access statements match current venue information? The advertisement should intrigue without falsifying the experience or exhausting every discovery.

## Adapt a structure instead of obeying it

Remove any beat that adds no meaning; combine functions when one shot can do both. Preserve an intentional long take or silence when it serves the brief. If the material cannot support the premise, obtain better material or return a premise revision to the screenwriter through the orchestrator. Never use a formula as an excuse to replace accepted user choices, fabricate evidence, substitute a narrated slideshow, or stop at a plan when the user requested a finished video.
