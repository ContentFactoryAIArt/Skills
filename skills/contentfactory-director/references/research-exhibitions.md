# Research: advertising contemporary exhibitions

Research checked 2026-11-13. This is the evidence behind the dedicated exhibition formula, not a claim that any campaign was produced or that its conversion rate was measured. Sources were read as web text; embedded films were not watched continuously. The synthesis and proposed editorial structures below are original recommendations, not instructions attributed to the institutions.

## What is different about selling an exhibition visit?

The deliverable is a promise about a future physical experience. An attractive object montage may fail to explain why someone should travel, what they will encounter, whether they can attend, or how to reserve. Conversely, an information card can explain admission while giving no reason to care. The creative task is to connect an encounter with a usable invitation.

Contemporary does not mean neon, projection, glitch transitions or an electronic soundtrack. Contemporary painting, a historical collection with a new curatorial argument, a design fair, a participatory installation and an industrial trade show can each demand a different visual language. The work, curatorial proposition and audience determine the treatment. Do not make every exhibition resemble an immersive digital attraction.

Three cases show materially different promises:

- **Participatory/immersive art:** the visitor's position and movement can change the experience. A comprehensible relation between person, space and work matters more than a succession of spectacular close-ups. The visitor is not automatically allowed to touch the work.
- **Curated art/design exhibition:** a compelling question and the relationship between selected works can carry the invitation. Artist, work, material and scale need enough clarity to remain identifiable. Sensational effects must not misrepresent the work's appearance.
- **Professional exhibition/trade show:** the useful encounter may be a demonstration, a supplier comparison, technical learning or a booked meeting. The audience needs the relevant specialty and entry conditions; generic crowd footage proves none of those benefits.

### EX01 — teamLab Borderless Tokyo, official visitor information

https://www.teamlab.art/e/tokyo/

Type: artist/institution's own experience and visitor rules; continuously updated, no single publication date shown. Read 2026-11-13.

Evidence: the museum describes a mapless experience; its app supports interpretation and participation in a named work. Commercial photography requires prior consent, even though personal photography and social posting are generally allowed. The page distinguishes timed entry, changing prices and practical visitor conditions.

Decision implication: establish what the actual visitor can do; show spatial scale and a real encounter. Verify commercial capture rights separately from public social availability. Retrieve live entry information for a real campaign rather than copying this research snapshot.

Limit: institutional description, not independent evidence of campaign effectiveness or a license to reuse artwork. Do not generalize one venue's conditions to other exhibitions.

## Proposed formula and its limits

**Encounter → curatorial/professional meaning → evidence of the visit → practical invitation.**

Start with an actual work, action or question that gives this exhibition an identity. Reveal enough space and human scale to make the experience legible. Connect selected encounters through the exhibition's idea or the professional visitor's task. Close with the correct event/edition, location, dates and useful booking or meeting action. For a very short placement, some functions can share a shot; there is no universal number of shots or fixed allocation of seconds.

For an art exhibition, the middle can be a material detail, a person encountering the whole work, and a contrasting work that changes the meaning of the first. For a trade show, it can be a practical problem, an actual demonstration and a relevant conversation or technical session. These are alternatives, not a demand to mix both audiences in one film.

Before filming, resolve artwork and music clearances, visitor releases where needed, venue equipment restrictions and whether a performance is scheduled or continuously available. If only archival footage exists, identify its edition and avoid implying that depicted installations or speakers are confirmed for the next one. Generated concept imagery is a proposal, not documentary evidence of a real room, artwork, exhibitor, crowd or testimonial.

## Original illustrative treatment: a 25-second design exhibition invitation

This invented example is a planning demonstration, not a real event or a measured best practice. Suppose authorized footage shows a kinetic sculpture, the maker explaining a material choice, and visitors looking at a static object. The confirmed curatorial theme is how everyday materials change our perception. Venue/date/ticket details are still absent.

| Provisional time | Picture and direction | Sound/text function | Why this beat exists |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0–5 s | Begin with the kinetic object's movement; widen enough to reveal scale. Let one movement complete. | Use its real mechanical sound if usable; identify the exhibition's question concisely. | An encounter specific to the show, with a readable payoff. |
| 5–11 s | Material detail followed by the maker's hands indicating the same feature; retain correct attribution. | One complete, verified phrase from the maker, or a restrained caption. | Converts spectacle into a curatorial idea. |
| 11–18 s | A visitor changes viewpoint around the static object; reveal the relationship between object and space. | Room sound and a musical change if justified; no invented reaction quote. | Demonstrates how looking can be an activity without fabricating interactivity. |
| 18–25 s | Return to a recognizable work with uncluttered invitation area. | Reserve readable space for confirmed title, venue/city, dates and ticket destination. | Converts interest into a feasible visit. |

Until the missing logistics are verified, deliver this as a treatment with unresolved fields; do not render a publishable invitation containing invented information. Timing must be adjusted to actual footage, speech, reading speed and placement. A 10-second version should preserve a coherent encounter and invitation rather than compress every shot mechanically.

## Evaluation questions specific to exhibitions

Can a viewer say what distinguishes this show and what they would actually do or see? Does the edit preserve the work's intended appearance and attribution? Is scale/location legible? Does the footage belong to the advertised edition? Are the date, eligibility and destination correct? Can the intended audience read the invitation at delivery size? Are sensory intensity and access statements accurate? Does urgency come from a real closing date or booking condition, rather than an invented shortage?
